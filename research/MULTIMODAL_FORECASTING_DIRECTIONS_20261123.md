# Can the wording of a subway alert improve next-hour travel forecasts across modes?

**Decision note · 23 November 2026 · Proposed pilot.** The literature and the previous study's reported scores have been reviewed. The new data products have been identified. No joined sample, Qwen forecasts or outcomes from this pilot are available yet.

## Why change the question?

Zhang et al. built LLM-UTP for short-term prediction across many traffic settings, with trend enhancement, spatial and temporal features, and a language-model backbone. They evaluated 11 datasets from 29 cities or areas. Our last experiment addressed a narrower question about transfer with scarce target observations. Its registered comparison tied at displayed precision. A secondary weighting rule gained 0.79 percentage points of local MAE against the local forecast, and the original released checkpoint had lower MAE on common observations in each of six domains. Training access differed. These data support closing the threshold search and trying a different source of predictive information. [Zhang et al., Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73610-2) · [Job 3111109 and common-row scores](../results/heldout/3111109/README.md)

## Recommended question and forecast

When a public notice first reports a service change, does understanding which stations trains will skip, terminate at or serve as alternatives improve the next hour's forecast of **subway entries and bike departures** in the same areas? Example invented for illustration — two notices can mention the same line and stations, while one instructs passengers to change trains at a station and another says trains will skip it. A station-name counter would treat those mentions alike.

Use one record per NYC taxi zone, forecast origin and mode. Forecast counts for the next complete clock hour. Target hours starting two and five hours later give the three- and six-hour horizons. Keep subway and bike counts separate. A later taxi arm would forecast yellow-taxi pickups in the same zones. Count forecasts cannot identify individual travelers switching modes.

## One experiment in two days

| Elapsed time | Work | Observable output |
|---|---|---|
| 0–6 hours | Inspect a January 2026 sample. Reconstruct the first published alert version for individual incidents. Map station complexes and bike trip origins onto the same taxi-zone map. Manually trace five incidents from notice to target hour. | Input field map, as-of timestamps, join rates, event counts and five traceable examples. If the publication time or zone join cannot be established, stop this pilot at feasibility. |
| 6–16 hours | Aggregate January–May. Use January–April for fitting and May for validation. Select the shared station/zone universe and coverage rules using training data only. Build seasonal and boosted numerical forecasts and the simple alert parser. | Chronological panel, source manifest, missingness report, forecasts and error by mode. |
| 16–30 hours | Run Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct once per distinct alert text and cache its output. Train the same numerical model with extracted station roles. Run the name-only, proximity and shuffled-role comparisons on the same cases. | Validation effect, each mode's result, event count, cost and parser failures. |
| 30–48 hours | Freeze code, prompt, data hashes and decision rule. If feasibility and validation support continuation, evaluate June 2026 once. Otherwise record the validation or feasibility result and close this experiment. | One decision table. Include elapsed time, inputs actually available and unresolved checks. |

Public inputs are the [MTA's versioned service alert archive](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/mta-service-alerts-beginning-april-2020), [subway hourly station-complex ridership](https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/mta-subway-hourly-ridership-beginning-2025), [Citi Bike trip histories](https://citibikenyc.com/system-data) and [NYC TLC zone polygons and optional yellow-taxi records](https://www.nyc.gov/site/tlc/about/tlc-trip-record-data.page). Verify version timestamps against the archive's definitions and contemporary service notices. The GTFS specification distinguishes the creation time, update time and active period. [MTA feed documentation](https://www.mta.info/document/90881)

**Data construction.** Fix the origin at the next hourly boundary following the first public notice for each event. Use only notice versions published by the origin and completed demand intervals before it. For the main role analysis use the first notice, keeping the latest publicly known update at the origin as a separately identified sensitivity. Keep contemporaneous line information, calendar, recent counts and the same local hour from previous weeks available to each applicable model. Track operating/coverage gaps separately from measured zeros. Disambiguate daylight-saving hours, station aliases and line direction. Sum the ridership measure once over disjoint fare categories. Keep bike origins and subway entries separate. Freeze a location crosswalk, document unmapped and ambiguous names, and treat overlapping zone-hours once. Assign whole related incidents to one time split and purge forecast windows spanning a split. Public historical trip files arrive in batches. The recent-count input assumes operator access to timely aggregates. Also score with recent counts removed or delayed to documented publication dates and label the information regime.

**Forecast population.** Use all qualifying subway service-change incidents with a known line from the as-of metadata. Define exposed zones from a fixed map of that line's stations, independently of the text model. Keep model evaluation pairs fixed even when Qwen cannot parse the notice. Select zones with both modes using only training coverage. Keep all eligible hours in a secondary evaluation. Report how many incidents, zones and dates remain, how many station roles are actually distinguishable at zone resolution, and how many incidents map to the same zones across hours. If station roles collapse at that resolution, record a feasibility failure before judging accuracy. Mask horizon-specific missing targets identically for every arm.

**Comparisons.** Use a weekly seasonal forecast and one shared gradient-boosted tree model with mode and zone identifiers. Compare (1) past counts and calendar; (2) add native alert fields, a dictionary/rule parser using the same notice text, directional templates, station mentions and distances; (3) add Qwen's station-role fields to (2). Validate a role-blind Qwen variant that keeps its same station names and distances but removes the roles. A shuffled-role control exchanges roles among the same mentioned stations within a notice where at least two roles exist. Count eligible notices separately. Preserve the numerical head, feature availability, training budget and scoring rows for all comparisons. Use the strongest eligible non-Qwen arm from validation as the locked practical comparator. Test Chronos-2 on the same numeric histories for the reference table, reporting any covariate or context restrictions. Train an exact reference-protocol LLM-UTP comparator if making an original-benchmark claim.

**What Qwen does.** A fixed prompt extracts route and direction, affected segment or station names, role (skipped, terminus, alternative, other or unknown), explicitly stated time window and supporting text span. Resolve names against the line and station dictionary. Ignore unsupported fields and record unknowns. The numerical model learns the size and sign of the response for each mode. Audit extraction on a random prespecified validation sample without displaying ridership outcomes. Keep rule/Qwen disagreements and unsupported assertions. Describe any annotation as internal. Only test 72B after a positive 7B signal or a documented extraction issue. On the same prespecified alerts, measure errors and incremental forecasting value on identical rows as well as GPU time. [Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct)

**Scoring and decision.** For each mode calculate MAE in trips or entries, then compute the percentage MAE reduction against the locked comparator on identical one-hour-ahead exposed zone-hours. Average the two percentage reductions equally. As a proposed investment threshold, continue to replication if the validation gain averages at least 3%, both modes improve, and equal-mode error across all hours worsens by at most 1% against the same comparator. Freeze those values before seeing new results. Report every arm, horizon and mode including losses. On the later test, give paired 95% intervals using contiguous calendar-week blocks that keep zones and modes together, event counts and weekly effects. A month supplies few independent weeks. Treat that result as a pilot. For a paper, extend untouched follow-up time and test held-out corridors or another city. Model-specific station-role claims require the full parser to improve on the role-blind parser too. A gain shared by both parsers supports using alerts as inputs. Neither finding measures causal mode switching.

## Fastest option on the original benchmark

If the primary objective is a direct numerical LLM forecast on the reference data, start with PEMS08 road counts and Beijing Metro ridership. Predict the next recorded interval with one shared protocol. Use the same chronological training data, context, features and output head budget for four cells: compact patch model or pretrained Qwen2.5-1.5B with a numerical head, each with raw levels or seasonal deviations as input and target. Give both representations the same past observations, baseline and scale. For the seasonal version subtract a per-location median of previous matching time slots and divide by a robust positive scale estimated from training history, invert the transform before scoring, and report MAE in original units. Compute daily/weekly features only where real times and uninterrupted intervals are verified. Include seasonal naïve, boosting and Chronos-2. Keep direct comparisons with LLM-UTP on the same physical observations, forecast horizon and training access. If both architectures benefit equally, the evidence supports preprocessing. A pretraining claim additionally needs a matched randomly initialized backbone control. Treat familiar benchmark dates as development evidence and reserve fresh dates or a new city for confirmation.

## What would warrant a paper?

The two-day deliverable is a feasibility and forecasting decision. A credible paper would show that knowing the roles of stations in a first notice improves forecasts beyond a strong text-aware parser and proximity, that the gain survives new incidents and corridors, and that it holds across transport modes with an explicit input-latency assumption. This result would answer a different question from LLM-UTP. A numerical-superiority statement about Zhang et al. needs a matched evaluation on their task. If simple preprocessing wins in the direct experiment, report that finding on its own terms.
