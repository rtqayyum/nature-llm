# Can the wording of a subway alert improve next-hour travel forecasts across modes?

**Research design · 23 November 2026.** The author prioritizes the quality of the result over a 48-hour deadline. The proposed study tests whether information about disrupted stations improves one-hour-ahead subway-entry and bike-departure forecasts. The NYC archive contains identifiable first notices and revisions. A candidate Boston archive has suitable documented fields. Both cities still need timestamp, geographic and target-coverage checks on joined data. Forecast accuracy is unmeasured.

## Why change the question?

Zhang et al. built LLM-UTP for short-term prediction across many traffic settings, with trend enhancement, spatial and temporal features, and a language-model backbone. They evaluated 11 datasets from 29 cities or areas. Our last experiment addressed a narrower question about transfer with scarce target observations. Its registered comparison tied at displayed precision. A secondary weighting rule gained 0.79 percentage points of local MAE against the local forecast, and the original released checkpoint had lower MAE on common observations in each of six domains. Training access differed. These data support closing the threshold search and trying a different source of predictive information. [Zhang et al., Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-73610-2) · [Job 3111109 and common-row scores](../results/heldout/3111109/README.md)

## Recommended question and forecast

When a public notice first reports a service change, does understanding which stations trains will skip, terminate at or serve as alternatives improve the next hour's forecast of **subway entries and bike departures** in the same areas? Example invented for illustration — two notices can mention the same line and stations, while one instructs passengers to change trains at a station and another says trains will skip it. A station-name counter would treat those mentions alike.

For the two-city study, use one record per station complex, forecast origin and mode. Count measured subway entries at the complex and bike departures at nearby docks. On a dated map, assign each dock to at most one complex using its nearest eligible complex within a proposed 500 m radius; fix the rule and a deterministic tie break before fitting. Check coverage and sensitivity to 300 m and 800 m on development data. This resolves the distinction between different stops that can fall in the same taxi zone. NYC taxi zones remain useful as a coarser sensitivity analysis and, separately, for a possible yellow-taxi outcome. Forecast counts for the first complete clock hour after the notice. Target hours starting two and five hours after that window starts give the three- and six-hour endpoints. Count forecasts alone cannot identify individual travelers switching modes.

**A verified NYC example.** MTA event 262675 on 11 August 2026 has an initial F-line notice (alert 537949) and a revision ten minutes later (537951). Both records have the same `affected` value and `delays` label. The first names Church Avenue as the far end of the express section from Kings Highway. The revision replaces Church Avenue with 18 Avenue. A pipeline using the final version would give the earlier forecast information it could not have had. Other inspected initial notices include 262344 (A train, alternative E service), 262324 (7 train, alternate direction), 262272 (N/Q express) and 262722 (Q delay, later expanded to both directions). These are source inspections, not five completed joins or a representative prevalence estimate. Mark 8–12 August 2026 NYC as exposed for development because the source page covers those days. **Data construction.** Issue the primary forecast at the first public notice for an unscheduled incident and predict the next complete clock hour. Use the first notice and demand intervals completed and accessible by its actual publication time. Record time from issue to target start. An updated forecast issued after a new notice is a secondary comparison with its own origin. Evaluate planned notices separately at a prespecified lead time relative to the announced start. Exclude later edits and observed end times from earlier inputs. Keep contemporaneous line information, calendar, recent counts and the same local hour from previous weeks available to each applicable model. Track operating/coverage gaps separately from measured zeros. Disambiguate daylight-saving hours, station aliases and line direction. Sum the ridership measure once over disjoint fare categories. Keep bike origins and subway entries separate. Freeze a location crosswalk, document unmapped and ambiguous names, and treat overlapping station-hours once. Assign whole related incidents to one time split and purge forecast windows spanning a split. Public historical trip files arrive in batches. The recent-count input assumes operator access to timely aggregates. Also score with recent counts removed or delayed to documented publication dates and label the information regime.

**Forecast population.** For each qualifying unscheduled incident with a known line, define the primary set of exposed station complexes from the as-of line map, independently of Qwen. Keep the same cases when an extractor returns unknown. Deduplicate overlapping forecasts using one prespecified origin rule (earliest eligible notice per complex and target hour) and report the overlap rate. Keep multiple incidents in the same time block when estimating uncertainty. Train-only coverage rules determine eligibility for both modes. Also report ordinary hours on an hourly forecast schedule with the same inputs and latency. Break down entity coverage, distinct event counts, role distinguishability and valid target coverage by mode. Mask missing targets identically for all comparisons.

**Comparisons.** Use a weekly seasonal forecast and one shared gradient-boosted tree model with mode and station-complex identifiers. Compare (1) past counts and calendar; (2) add native alert fields, a dictionary/rule parser using the same notice text, directional templates, station mentions and distances; (3) add Qwen's station-role fields to (2). Validate a role-blind Qwen variant that keeps its same station names and distances but removes the roles. A shuffled-role control exchanges roles among the same mentioned stations within a notice where at least two roles exist. Count eligible notices separately. Preserve the numerical head, feature availability, training budget and scoring rows for all comparisons. Use the strongest eligible non-Qwen arm from validation as the locked practical comparator. Test Chronos-2 on the same numeric histories for the reference table, reporting any covariate or context restrictions. Train an exact reference-protocol LLM-UTP comparator if making an original-benchmark claim.

**What Qwen does.** A fixed prompt extracts route and direction, affected segment or station names, role (skipped, terminus, alternative, other or unknown), explicitly stated time window and supporting text span. Resolve names against the line and station dictionary. Ignore unsupported fields and record unknowns. The numerical model learns the size and sign of the response for each mode. Audit extraction on a random prespecified validation sample without displaying ridership outcomes. Keep rule/Qwen disagreements and unsupported assertions. Describe any annotation as internal. Only test 72B after a positive 7B signal or a documented extraction issue. On the same prespecified alerts, measure errors and incremental forecasting value on identical rows as well as GPU time. [Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct)

**Scoring and decision.** The primary contrast is equal-mode percentage MAE reduction for Qwen's full role features versus the strongest validation-selected non-Qwen baseline, measured on identical one-hour-ahead exposed station-hours. The mechanism contrast retains Qwen's station identities and drops their roles. Report both with MAE in trips/entries, per city and mode. The earlier 3% pilot improvement and 1% all-hours deterioration values are planning benchmarks. They can inform a sample-size calculation using development data. They do not establish significance or publication readiness. Freeze the smallest practically useful effect and a precision target before opening the confirmatory data, and report null or harmful effects. Estimate paired 95% uncertainty by resampling contiguous time blocks, preserving together all simultaneous stations, modes and incidents. Choose block length from development dependence diagnostics and report a prespecified sensitivity. Measure performance on ordinary hours as a secondary endpoint, with three- and six-hour results and corridor breakdowns. Record how many independent blocks and distinct incidents support each comparison. Restrict generalization claims to the populations actually tested.

## Fastest option on the original benchmark

If the primary objective is a direct numerical LLM forecast on the reference data, start with PEMS08 road counts and Beijing Metro ridership. Predict the next recorded interval with one shared protocol. Use the same chronological training data, context, features and output head budget for four cells: compact patch model or pretrained Qwen2.5-1.5B with a numerical head, each with raw levels or seasonal deviations as input and target. Give both representations the same past observations, baseline and scale. For the seasonal version subtract a per-location median of previous matching time slots and divide by a robust positive scale estimated from training history, invert the transform before scoring, and report MAE in original units. Compute daily/weekly features only where real times and uninterrupted intervals are verified. Include seasonal naïve, boosting and Chronos-2. Keep direct comparisons with LLM-UTP on the same physical observations, forecast horizon and training access. If both architectures benefit equally, the evidence supports preprocessing. A pretraining claim additionally needs a matched randomly initialized backbone control. Treat familiar benchmark dates as development evidence and reserve fresh dates or a new city for confirmation.

## What would warrant a paper?

A paper aimed at *Nature Communications* would need evidence that station roles in first notices add information beyond strong text-aware and spatial baselines, that this benefit survives untouched events and corridors, and that independent city data support a reproducible claim. Publish the forecast-origin benchmark, parser protocol, nonidentifying joins, paired predictions or reproducible evaluation where the data terms permit, and the internal statistical review. If a simpler parser matches Qwen on adequately sized tests, report the simpler result. If evidence is imprecise, report its range and avoid claiming superiority. A numerical-superiority statement about Zhang et al. still needs a matched evaluation on their task.

If simple preprocessing wins in the direct experiment, report that finding on its own terms.
