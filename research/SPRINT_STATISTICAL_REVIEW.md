## Probability argument and statistical scope

For a fixed pair, let L, T and s be known at issue time. The target label matures before the next one-step comparison. The reverse triangle inequality bounds the absolute scaled MAE difference D by b = mean_nodes |T−L|/s before consulting the label. Nodes do not increase the count of sequential observations. Conditional Hoeffding on an interval of length 2b gives log-mgf at most λ²b²/2 for the centered increment. Multiplication over time, Gaussian mixing with precision ρ, and Ville's inequality give the registered half-width sqrt((V+ρ) log((V+ρ)/(ρδ²)))/n, where V is the sum of b². Its upper endpoint must be strictly smaller than −0.01. The scale is determined from support. The original two-sided bound applies to one stream's average conditional loss difference through an observed verification prefix under the ordered-row feedback assumption. Neither test-block performance nor arbitrary future drift receives a confidence guarantee. The one-sided directional comparison has its own null/mixture interpretation; the empirical full-window, two-thirds and blend rules carry empirical outcomes only.

The registered analysis uses per-stream δ = 0.05. Four policies applied to a shared pair, three training arms, paired seeds and a shared NYC source fold cannot be counted as independent trials. The 95% paired test-row intervals describe uncertainty conditional on six datasets. Temporal dependence and few blocks (especially Hangzhou) limit how strongly we can interpret them.
